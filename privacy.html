Daily English

Privacy Policy

Effective date: 14 October 2026

Daily English (“the app”) is an English-learning app for children and adults, published by Harendra Kushwah (“we”, “us”). This policy explains what information the app uses, why, and the choices you have.

The short version

  • We don’t have accounts or logins, and we never see your name. Everything you enter stays on your phone.
  • The app sends anonymous usage statistics and crash reports to help us improve it. You can turn these off in Settings.
  • The app shows ads from Google AdMob. In kid mode, every ad request is marked as child-directed.
  • Your voice is never sent to us. Speaking practice uses your phone’s own speech service.
  • We don’t sell your information.

1. Information that stays on your device

The app saves the following only on your phone, in the app’s private storage. We never receive it.

  • The name you type during setup (optional), the buddy picture you choose, and kid or adult mode.
  • Your learning progress: finished lessons, stars, XP, coins, hearts, streaks, quests and game scores.
  • Your settings, such as sound effects and the daily reminder time.

You can erase your progress at any time in Settings → Reset progress. Deleting the app deletes everything it saved on your phone.

2. Information collected by services we use

The app uses a few Google services. They collect the information below on our behalf, or on their own as described in their policies.

Service What it collects Why
Google Analytics for Firebase How the app is used, such as screens opened, lessons started and finished, quiz and game scores, coins earned and spent, and which reward buttons are tapped. It also collects a random app-instance ID, device model, operating system, app version, language and approximate country (from your IP address). To understand which parts of the app help people learn and where they get stuck.
Firebase Crashlytics Crash reports: what the app was doing when it crashed, the last screens opened, device model, operating system, free memory and storage, and a random installation ID. To find and fix bugs.
Firebase Performance Monitoring How long things take in the app, such as start-up time, screen freezes, ad loading and network requests (web addresses without their query details), along with device model, operating system, app version, network type, approximate country and a random installation ID. To find and fix slow parts of the app.
Firebase Remote Config and A/B Testing A random installation ID, app version, language and country, used to download app settings (such as shop prices or how often ads appear) and to place devices in test groups. To improve the app by changing settings without an update and comparing versions.
Google AdMob Device information, IP address, ad interactions and, in teen & adult mode, your device’s advertising ID if your device settings and consent choices allow it. To show ads, including optional ads you choose to watch for rewards, and to measure them.

We have set these services up to collect as little as possible:

  • Analytics, Crashlytics and Performance Monitoring never receive your name, your typed answers or your voice.
  • Analytics does not collect the advertising ID, and we have turned off ad personalisation and advertising features for Analytics.
  • We don’t use analytics or crash data for advertising, and we don’t combine it with anything that identifies you. The ad SDK counts anonymous ad views and clicks in Analytics so we can see how ads perform.

Speaking practice and listening

When you tap the microphone, your phone’s built-in speech recognition turns your voice into text so the app can check your pronunciation. This is Apple’s speech recognition on iPhone and Google’s speech service on Android. Depending on your phone, your voice may be processed by Apple or Google under their privacy policies. The app does not record, store or send your voice or the recognised text to us. Words are read aloud by your phone’s own text-to-speech.

Reminders

Daily reminders are scheduled on your phone. No server is involved, and you can turn them off in Settings or in your phone’s notification settings.

3. Children

Daily English has a kid mode for young learners. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

  • A child’s name and buddy picture stay on the device and are never sent to us.
  • In kid mode, every ad request is marked as directed to children and under the age of consent, and limited to ads rated suitable for general audiences (G). Google then turns off personalised ads and limits the data it uses for those requests.
  • Kid mode has no full-screen ads between activities. Optional reward ads play only when tapped.
  • Analytics never collects the advertising ID, in any mode.

Parents can turn off usage and crash data in Settings → Share usage & crash data, or contact us with any question.

4. Your choices

  • Usage and crash data: turn off Settings → Share usage & crash data. The app then stops sending analytics, crash reports and performance data.
  • Ads: in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, the app asks for your consent before showing personalised ads. On any device you can reset or delete your advertising ID, or limit ad tracking, in your phone’s settings.
  • Microphone: you can deny or remove microphone access at any time. Speaking practice is optional.
  • Your progress: use Reset progress, or delete the app.

5. How long information is kept

  • Information on your device is kept until you reset your progress or delete the app.
  • Analytics data is kept for 2 months, after which Google deletes it.
  • Crash reports are kept for 90 days, and performance data for up to 90 days.
  • AdMob data is kept according to Google’s policies.

6. Sharing

We don’t sell or rent your information. Information is only processed by the service providers named above (Google), who act on our instructions or under their own policies:

  • Google Privacy Policy
  • Privacy and security in Firebase
  • How Google uses information from apps that use its services

We may also disclose information if the law requires it.

7. Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR in Europe or the CCPA in California), you may have the right to access, correct or delete your information, or to object to its use. Because the app has no accounts and we never receive your name, we usually can’t identify which analytics data belongs to you. Turning off usage data and deleting the app stops all further collection. For any request, contact us below and we’ll help as far as we can.

8. Security

Information sent to Google’s services is encrypted in transit. Information on your device is protected by your phone’s own security.

9. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we’ll update it on this page and change the effective date above. Significant changes will also be mentioned in the app’s release notes.
